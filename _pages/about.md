Dr. Destynie Medeiros studies how distinct experiences are encoded, recalled, updated, and integrated across time, and how trauma reshapes these processes. In Dr. Denise Cai’s lab, a leader in Miniscope development and calcium imaging, she combines simultaneous in vivo Miniscope calcium imaging and electrophysiology to examine how ensembles of hippocampal neurons coordinate to bind experiences and guide future behavior, including social interactions.

<img src="/images/image1.jpg" alt="Destynie Medeiros presenting a research poster" style="float: right; clear: right; width: 150px; height: 150px; object-fit: cover; margin: 0.3em 0 1em 1.5em; border-radius: 8px;">

Destynie received her Ph.D. in Neurobiology from the University of Alabama at Birmingham, where she trained with Dr. Lucas Pozzo-Miller. Her doctoral research examined social memory, the ability to recognize, remember, and respond to individuals, in a mouse model of Rett syndrome, identifying molecular, synaptic, and population-level abnormalities that alter coordinated neural activity during behavior. This work motivated her to understand how the brain represents social experiences and how disruptions in these processes lead to behavioral deficits. She received her B.A. in Biology from the University of Hartford, where she completed her honors thesis research with Dr. Paola Sacchetti. Destynie's honors thesis work asked how probiotic supplementation may alleviate cellular and behavioral changes in a mouse model for Alzheimer's disease.
