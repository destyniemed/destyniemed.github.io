Dr. Destynie Medeiros is a neuroscientist who studies memory: how we form it, how we hold on to it, and how difficult experiences can change it. Her work connects social behavior, trauma, and the neural circuits that link experiences across time.

In Dr. Denise Cai’s lab at the Icahn School of Medicine, a leader in Miniscope development and calcium imaging, Destynie is conducting her postdoctoral research which combines in vivo Miniscope calcium imaging and electrophysiology to examine how ensembles of neurons coordinate to bind experiences and guide future behavior, including social interactions.

<img src="/images/image1.jpg" alt="Destynie Medeiros presenting a research poster" style="float: right; clear: right; width: 150px; height: 150px; object-fit: cover; margin: 0.3em 0 1em 1.5em; border-radius: 8px;">

Destynie received her Ph.D. in Neurobiology from the University of Alabama at Birmingham, where she trained with Dr. Lucas Pozzo-Miller. Her doctoral research examined social memory, the ability to recognize, remember, and respond to individuals, in a mouse model of Rett syndrome, identifying molecular, synaptic, and population-level abnormalities that alter coordinated neural activity during behavior. This work motivated her to understand how the brain represents social experiences and how disruptions in these processes lead to behavioral deficits. 

Destynie received her B.A. in Biology from the University of Hartford, where she completed her honors thesis research with Dr. Paola Sacchetti. Destynie's honors thesis work asked how probiotic supplementation may alleviate cellular and behavioral changes in a mouse model for Alzheimer's disease. 

<img src="/images/image2.png" alt="New York Memory Hub logo" style="float: right; clear: right; width: 150px; height: 150px; object-fit: contain; margin: 0.3em 0 1em 1.5em;">

Beyond research, Destynie is active in teaching, service, and inclusion initiatives. She founded and chairs the New York Memory Hub’s Junior Executive Committee.
